# Personal Investment & Savings Strategies

## Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)
A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a method of investing a fixed sum of money regularly (weekly, monthly, or quarterly) into a mutual fund scheme rather than making a lump-sum payment.
* **Benefits of SIP**:
  - **Rupee Cost Averaging**: You buy more mutual fund units when the market price is low and fewer units when the price is high. This averages out the cost of acquisition over time.
  - **Disciplined Savings**: Instills regular financial discipline as funds are auto-debited.
  - **Power of Compounding**: Regular, long-term investments allow interest earned to earn further interest.

## Mutual Funds: Equity vs Debt
Mutual funds pool money from multiple investors to invest in securities.
* **Equity Mutual Funds**: Invest primarily in stocks/shares of companies.
  - **Risk**: High risk, short-term volatility.
  - **Returns**: Potential for high returns, recommended for long-term goals (5+ years).
* **Debt Mutual Funds**: Invest in fixed-income securities like government bonds, corporate debentures, and treasury bills.
  - **Risk**: Low to moderate risk.
  - **Returns**: Moderate and relatively stable returns, recommended for short-to-medium-term goals (1-3 years).

## Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS)
ELSS is a specific type of diversified equity mutual fund that offers tax benefits.
* **Tax Benefit**: Eligible for deduction up to ₹1.5 Lakhs under Section 80C.
* **Lock-in Period**: Has a mandatory lock-in period of 3 years, which is the shortest lock-in period among all Section 80C options (e.g. PPF has 15 years, Tax Saving FD has 5 years).
