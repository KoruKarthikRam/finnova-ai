# Indian Taxation System Guide

## Old vs New Tax Regime
In India, taxpayers can choose between the Old Tax Regime and the New Tax Regime.
* **Old Tax Regime**: Offers higher tax rates but allows numerous exemptions and deductions under Section 80C, 80D, HRA, and LTA.
* **New Tax Regime**: Offers lower tax rates across simplified tax slabs but rejects most deductions and exemptions (except for standard deduction of ₹75,000 as of recent budget rules).

## Section 80C Deductions
Section 80C is the most popular tax-saving section under the Old Tax Regime.
* **Maximum Limit**: The total deduction limit under Section 80C is ₹1,50,000 (1.5 Lakhs) per financial year.
* **Eligible Schemes**:
  - Employee Provident Fund (EPF) and Public Provident Fund (PPF).
  - National Savings Certificate (NSC).
  - Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) mutual funds.
  - Life Insurance premium payments.
  - Principal repayment on Home Loans.
  - Tuition fees for up to two children.
  - Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY).

## Section 80D Medical Insurance
Section 80D provides deduction on premiums paid for medical insurance.
* **Self, Spouse, and Children**: Up to ₹25,000 per year (₹50,000 if self/spouse are senior citizens).
* **Parents**: Additional deduction of up to ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if parents are senior citizens).
* **Preventive Health Checkup**: Includes up to ₹5,000 within the overall limits.

## House Rent Allowance (HRA) Tax Exemption
Taxpayers living in rented accommodation can claim HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act.
* **Exemption Amount**: The exemption is the minimum of the following three values:
  1. Actual HRA received from the employer.
  2. Rent paid minus 10% of basic salary.
  3. 50% of basic salary for metro cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) or 40% of basic salary for non-metro cities.
